How it works

1 · Measure the shift

Both images go through a Fourier transform. Dividing out the magnitude leaves only phase, and the inverse transform of that is a single sharp spike sitting exactly at the offset between them. It is called phase correlation, and because the magnitude is thrown away it is unbothered by one photo being brighter than the other.

2 · Line them up

The second image is resampled by the measured offset. Turn on rotation and scale and a second pass runs first, on a log-polar resampling of the spectrum, where a turn becomes a sideways shift and a zoom becomes a vertical one.

3 · Mark the difference

What remains after alignment is real change. Pixels that differ by more than the sensitivity threshold are painted red, with a majority filter removing the speckle that noise and resampling leave behind.
